Portfolio UX Redesign

Redesigning my portfolio to communicate my role, reasoning, and design process more clearly.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Methods
UX Evaluation, Information Architecture, Iterative Design
Tools
Figma, VS Code, Chrome DevTools
Timeline
3 Weeks
01

The Challenge

How can a portfolio help recruiters quickly understand not just what I designed, but how I think?

My original portfolio presented my projects, but it didn't communicate the story behind them clearly. Case studies relied heavily on screenshots and brief descriptions, making it difficult to understand the problems I was solving, the reasoning behind my decisions, or how my work connected to user needs.

I reframed the portfolio as a UX problem itself: how could the experience communicate my value as clearly as the projects it was presenting?

02

Evaluation

Reviewing the portfolio from first impression to project exploration.

I followed the same path a visitor would take from the homepage into individual projects, documenting where the experience failed to clearly communicate my role, process, and work.

First Impression

Reviewed what was immediately visible before interacting with the page.

Content Walkthrough

Followed the portfolio from homepage to project pages and noted where context or reasoning was missing.

Hierarchy Check

Compared what was visually emphasized with what was most important for understanding the work.

Navigation Check

Checked whether the interface made it clear where to go next and how to move between projects.

03

The Problem

The original portfolio made it difficult to understand my role, process, and value.

Unclear Positioning

My role and strengths weren't immediately apparent.

The landing experience didn't clearly communicate what I do, what kind of problems I solve, or what visitors should explore next.

Shallow Case Studies

Projects emphasized final screens over design reasoning.

Case studies showed the outcomes, but gave readers little visibility into the decisions and reasoning that produced them.

Weak Storytelling

Information lacked a clear narrative and hierarchy.

Without a clear progression from problem to process to outcome, readers had to piece together the story themselves.

04

Defining the Direction

The portfolio needed to shift from presenting work to communicating design thinking.

Rather than treating each finding as an isolated fix, I used them to define three priorities for the redesign: clarify my value, expose the reasoning behind my work, and create clearer paths into deeper project stories.

From

Presenting Work

  • Final screens
  • Brief descriptions
  • Passive browsing
→
Toward

Communicating Thinking

  • Decisions + reasoning
  • Structured narratives
  • Clear paths to explore

This shift became the lens for every design decision that followed.

05

Redesigning the Experience

Turning the strategy into a system.

I translated those priorities across three levels of the portfolio: the landing experience, individual project entry points, and the structure of the case studies themselves.

05.1

Landing Experience

Establishing a clear entry point

Role-First Introduction

The opening statement now communicates my UX focus immediately, replacing the ambiguous introduction of the previous portfolio.

Clear Action Hierarchy

A single primary CTA establishes the intended next step instead of asking visitors to interpret several competing options.

Featured Work Hierarchy

Projects are introduced as part of the homepage narrative, allowing visitors to evaluate the work before committing to a full case study.

05.2

Project Context

Making each project understandable at a glance

Opening a case study shouldn't require the reader to reconstruct what the project is. I redesigned the project introduction to answer the essential questions before the process begins.

Immediate Context

Project type and a concise description establish what the work is and why it matters before the reader enters the process.

Role & Approach

Role, methods, tools, and timeline make my contribution and approach visible without requiring the reader to search for context.

Visual Context

Supporting visuals are introduced where they add context to the project, helping readers connect the overview to the work without interrupting the narrative.

05.3

Case Study Structure

Making the reasoning as visible as the final design

The original case studies showed what I designed, but gave readers little insight into how I arrived there. I restructured each project around the decisions that shaped the outcome, creating a clearer progression from problem to evidence, reasoning, and design response.

Problem

Establish the primary challenge

Each case study begins by defining the problem and why it matters before presenting a solution.

→

Evidence

Show what informed the decision

Research, evaluation findings, and observations provide evidence for the problems being addressed.

→

Reasoning

Connect findings to decisions

Design choices are explained in relation to the findings instead of appearing as isolated visual changes.

→

Design Response

Show the resulting solution

Final designs demonstrate how the earlier reasoning was translated into a concrete response.

The goal wasn't to add more content. It was to make the relationship between the problem, my reasoning, and the resulting design easier to follow.

06

Reflection

The biggest redesign wasn't visual. It was how I communicated my thinking.

I began this project focused on improving the presentation of my portfolio, but evaluating the experience revealed a deeper problem: showing finished work wasn't enough if readers couldn't understand the decisions behind it.

Redesigning the portfolio pushed me to think more deliberately about what information a reader needs, when they need it, and how one piece of context should lead into the next. That changed both the structure of the portfolio and the way I approach design work.

The project reinforced that communicating a design decision clearly is part of the design itself.
