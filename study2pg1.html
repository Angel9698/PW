UX Case Study

Movie Theater Wayfinding

Physical Experience Usability Study

Evaluating how environmental cues and directional signage affect the mobile-order pickup experience.

Role
UX Researcher / Designer
Methods
Observational Research, Heuristic Evaluation
Focus
Wayfinding, Error Prevention, Physical UX
Timeline
2 Weeks
01

The Problem

Guests were following the directions— and still ending up in the wrong place.

A mobile-order system allowed moviegoers to purchase concessions ahead of time and collect them at a designated pickup area. However, I repeatedly observed guests waiting at a different pickup location and requiring employee assistance before reaching the correct destination.

Rather than treating this as user error, I investigated how the theater's signage and physical layout were shaping that behavior.

02

Observing the Behavior

I observed how guests responded after encountering the directional signage, looking for patterns in where they went next.

100% of first-time guests

Every first-time mobile-order guest I observed went to the incorrect pickup location.

Guests who moved directly to the correct location had been through the experience before, suggesting that success depended on prior knowledge rather than clear wayfinding.

03

Why the Experience Failed

Heuristic evaluation · 3 Nielsen heuristics · Severity scale: 1 (least severe) – 5 (most severe)

3 / 5 Severity 3 out of 5

Visibility of System Status

Guests couldn't tell which pickup area was meant for them.

The nearby sign simply said “Pick up here,” without distinguishing hot-food pickup from mobile-order pickup. Therefore, guests could reasonably believe they had already reached the correct destination.

2 / 5 Severity 2 out of 5

Match Between System and Real World

The mobile-order area was recognizable— but only once guests actually saw it.

The correct pickup area used language and visual cues consistent with the mobile experience. Earlier environmental cues, however, often directed guests somewhere else first.

5 / 5 Severity 5 out of 5

Error Prevention

The environment created the error instead of preventing it.

First-time guests formed the wrong expectation before reaching the correct pickup area and only discovered the mistake after waiting and being redirected.

04

Designing a Better Path

Preferred Direction

Make the correct destination visible before guests commit to the wrong one.

I prioritized changing the first directional cue rather than relocating the pickup operation itself.

Other directions considered

02

Lower Intervention

Clarify the existing pickup area.

Add a larger sign identifying the nearby counter as hot-food pickup and provide a clear direction toward the mobile-order area.

03

Higher Intervention

Relocate the mobile-order pickup area.

Move mobile pickup to the opposite side of the concession area and support the new location with additional signage. This could improve visibility, but would require more operational change and resources.
